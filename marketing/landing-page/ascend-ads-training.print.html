What this training covers, and the one idea the whole thing is built on.

Most academy owners think their ads failed because the video wasn’t good enough. It’s almost never the video. It’s the plumbing behind it — the wrong objective, no tracking, a slow page, a lead that sat unanswered for two days. That’s the delivery side, and that’s what this is.

The five layers of an ad that works

Every result you get is one of five layers doing its job or failing at it. Ads Manager will not tell you which one. Learn to read the numbers and you'll know in sixty seconds.

1

The offer

Whether anybody wants the thing you’re advertising. Fixed in Module 02.

2

The creative — already done

Whether they stop scrolling. You’ve built this. This workbook doesn’t re-teach it; it tells you where to load it and what breaks it.

3

The delivery

Who Meta shows it to, how often, and what it charges you. Modules 03 to 06.

4

The capture

Whether a click becomes a name and a phone number. Module 02.

5

The follow-up

Whether that name becomes a paying player. Module 09 — and this is where most of the money is lost.

01The plumbing

Do this once. 30–45 minutes. Skip a step here and you'll be debugging it for a month.

Meta gives you two different places to click. Meta Business Suite is the admin layer — assets, people, payments. Ads Manager is where campaigns get built. You need the first one set up properly before the second is worth touching.

A. Business portfolio & ad account

  1. Create a Business portfolioGo to business.facebook.com and create one in your academy's name, not your own. This is the container that owns everything. If you ever sell the academy, bring in staff, or hire help, you hand over this — not your personal login.
  2. Add your Page to itBusiness settings → Accounts → Pages → Add → Add an existing Page. If your Page is currently stuck under a personal profile, this is the step that frees it.
  3. Create an ad accountBusiness settings → Accounts → Ad accounts → Add → Create a new ad account. Name it ACADEMY NAME - Ads. Currency GBP. Time zone London.
  4. Currency and time zone are permanentYou cannot change them later. Changing your mind means a brand new ad account and losing every bit of history and learning attached to the old one. Read them twice before you click create.
  5. Add a payment methodBilling → Payment settings. Add a card, then set an account spending limit equal to your monthly budget while you're learning. It's a hard stop that protects you from a mis-typed daily budget.
  6. Turn on two-factor authenticationHacked ad accounts are common, and Meta support is close to non-existent when it happens. Put 2FA on the personal profile that administers the business portfolio. Now, not later.
  7. Add a second admin you trustIf you lose access to your profile, a second admin can restore yours. One person with sole access is a single point of failure for the whole marketing engine.

The most common blocker

New ad accounts sometimes launch straight into review, or get restricted for no obvious reason. If that happens: complete every identity verification Meta asks for, don't create a second account to get around it (that makes it worse), and use the Account Quality section to request a review. Most clear within a few days.

B. Tracking — the dataset (pixel)

Meta now calls the pixel a dataset. Same thing: a snippet of code on your funnel page that tells Meta “this person filled the form in”.

Without it, two things happen. Meta is guessing who to show your ads to instead of learning from real conversions — and you are flying blind on cost per lead. It is the single highest-value 20 minutes in this entire workbook.

  1. Create the datasetBusiness settings → Data sources → Datasets → Add. Name it ACADEMY NAME - Web. Copy the ID — a 15 or 16 digit number — and write it on the worksheet two pages on.
  2. Install it on your funnel pageASCEND trial funnel: paste the ID into the pixel field in your page setup. Player Portal: Settings → Marketing → Meta Pixel, paste, save — page views and lead events then fire automatically. Anything else: paste the base code into the <head> of every page.
  3. Wire the Lead eventThe form submission must fire a Lead event. This is the most important line of tracking you own: it's what “cost per lead” counts, and what Meta optimises delivery towards. Everything downstream is built on it.
  4. Test it before you spend anythingInstall the Meta Pixel Helper Chrome extension. Load your funnel page — it should show PageView. Submit a real test lead — it should show Lead. If it doesn't, stop and fix it. Do not launch on faith.
  5. Verify your domainBusiness settings → Brand safety → Domains. Add your domain and verify it, either with a DNS TXT record or a meta tag in your page head. This gives you control of your own conversion events and stops iOS privacy rules eating your reporting.
  6. Set Lead as a priority eventEvents Manager → your dataset → Aggregated Event Measurement → configure web events. Put Lead at the top of the list. Ignore this and conversions from iOS users may not report at all — which makes a working campaign look dead.
  7. Optional but worth it: the Conversions APIBrowser tracking gets blocked; server-side tracking doesn't. If your funnel tool offers a CAPI or server-events integration, switch it on. It typically recovers 10–25% of conversions that the browser pixel loses.

If you use Meta Instant Forms instead

You don't strictly need a pixel — the form lives on Facebook itself, so Meta sees the conversion natively. You still want the pixel installed for retargeting and for the day you switch to your own page. See Module 02: it's a real trade-off, not a shortcut.

02One offer, one destination

The two decisions that cap your results before the ad ever runs.

You are not advertising your academy. Nobody clicks “an academy”. You are advertising one specific thing a parent can say yes to this week — with a date, a place and an age group attached to it.

Offers that convert cold traffic

  • Free trial session — named date, named venue, named age group. The workhorse. Highest volume, lowest friction.
  • Holiday camp — deadline built in, parents are actively looking, easiest paid conversion of the year.
  • £10 taster block (2–3 weeks) — fewer leads than a free trial, far better show-up rate, because they've paid something.
  • New venue or new age group launch — genuine news. Works brilliantly for a 2–3 week burst.
  • Bring a friend week — cheap, and it converts through your existing parents.

Offers that don't

  • “Join our academy” — no date, no decision, nothing to say yes to.
  • “Sign up to our newsletter” — a parent whose child wants to play football does not want a newsletter.
  • Price as the headline, before they know who you are.
  • Three offers in one ad. One campaign, one offer. Always.
  • “Enquire now” — vague, and it attracts people with vague intent.

The four things every offer must state

ElementWeakStrong
What“Football sessions”“Free 60-minute trial session”
Who“Kids”“Boys and girls, ages 6–12, all abilities”
Where“Aberdeen”“Sports Village, Linksfield — free parking”
When“Weekly”“This Saturday, 9am”

Your offer, written out in full

Write it as you'd say it to a parent at the school gates. If it takes more than one breath, it's too complicated.

The ASCEND default: your funnel page

You pay a bit more per lead and you get a parent who actually turns up. Cost per enrolled player is the only number that pays your bills, and the funnel page usually wins it. Use Instant Forms when you're filling a camp fast, on a hard deadline, and raw volume beats quality.

If you do use an Instant Form

  • Choose “Higher intent”, not “More volume” — it adds a confirmation step that removes most accidental leads.
  • Add one custom question: “What age is your child?” with age-band options. It filters out half the junk and pre-qualifies the call.
  • Write a proper completion screen with your phone number and what happens next.
  • Wire the leads out to email or your CRM on day one, and test it with a real submission.

“Instant Forms look brilliant on the dashboard and average on the pitch. Forty cheap leads where twelve turn up is worse than twenty-five where sixteen turn up — and it's a lot more phone calls for you.”

Pre-flight your destination page

Open it on your own phone, on 4G with wifi switched off. That's how your parents will see it — not on your laptop on fast broadband.

  • It loads in under 3 seconds. Every extra second costs roughly 10% of your leads.
  • Offer, age group, venue and date are visible without scrolling.
  • The form asks for 4 fields maximum: parent name, child age, phone, email.
  • The phone field brings up a number keypad, not a full keyboard.
  • The button says something specific — “Book my free trial”, not “Submit”.
  • There is one call to action on the page. No menu, no other links, no distractions.
  • Photos are of your sessions, with consent on file. Not stock photos.
  • The page answers the three questions every parent has: how much, how old, where exactly.
  • You submitted a real test lead and it arrived on your phone.
  • The thank-you page says exactly what happens next: “I'll text you within the hour to confirm your spot.”
  • The wording on the page matches the ad. If the ad says Saturday 9am, the page says Saturday 9am.

The mismatch tax

The single most expensive mistake on this page is an ad that promises one thing and a page that talks about another. The parent's brain reads it as “wrong place” and they leave in under two seconds — and you paid for that click. Ad and page should read like one sentence.

03Build the campaign

Click by click. Meta moves its buttons — the functions stay the same, so learn what each setting does.

Ads Manager has three levels, and each controls something completely different. Get this straight in your head and the whole interface stops being confusing, permanently.

Campaign

The goal and the budget. What are you asking Meta to go and get?

Ad set

Who, where, how much, when. Audience, location, placements, schedule.

Ad

The creative. Video, text, headline, button, destination link.

The structure you're aiming for

CAMPAIGN — 2026-09 | Trial | Aberdeen U8-U12  (Leads, £15/day)
  └─ AD SET — Linksfield +10km | Parents 25-50
      ├─ AD 01 — Session footage / hook A
      ├─ AD 02 — Parent testimonial
      ├─ AD 03 — Coach to camera / hook B
      └─ AD 04 — Static image + offer

One campaign. One ad set. Three or four ads. That's it. Complexity is not sophistication at £15 a day — it just means less data in each box and slower learning.

Naming conventions that save you next year

LevelPatternExample
CampaignMonth | Offer | Area+Age2026-09 | Trial | Aberdeen U8-U12
Ad setVenue +radius | AudienceLinksfield +10km | Parents 25-50
AdFormat - hook - versionReel - GirlsScoring - v2

Sloppy names are the reason owners can't tell what worked last season. Ninety seconds now, an hour saved later.

The build, part one — campaign level

  1. Ads Manager → Create → objective LEADSNot Traffic. Not Engagement. Not the Boost button on your post. Leads tells Meta's algorithm to go and find people who fill forms in — not people who like things. This one dropdown is worth more than every other setting combined.
  2. Name it properlyUse the convention from the previous page. You will thank yourself in eleven months.
  3. Special ad category: leave it OFFThat setting is for housing, employment, credit and politics. Children's sport is none of those. Switching it on needlessly strips out your age targeting and widens your radius by force, which wrecks delivery and costs you money.
  4. Budget at campaign level (Advantage campaign budget)Set £10–15 a day to start. One budget shared across the campaign, with Meta moving spend to whatever is working. At this level it beats splitting the budget by hand, every time.
  5. Daily, not lifetimeDaily budget is easier to control and easier to scale in 20% steps. Use lifetime budget only for a camp campaign with a hard end date.
  6. Leave bid strategy on the defaultHighest volume (sometimes shown as “lowest cost”). Do not set a bid cap or cost cap while you're learning — caps stop delivery dead if they're set below what your auction actually costs, and you'll think the ad is broken.

Never use the Boost button

Boosting gives you the Engagement objective wearing a disguise. You get likes from people who will never enrol, no conversion optimisation, no proper targeting, and no usable reporting. It is the most expensive button on Facebook. Every campaign in this workbook is built in Ads Manager.

What “optimisation” actually means

Meta runs an auction for every single impression. It's not selling you space — it's deciding which advertiser gets to show something to this specific person, right now. When you choose Leads, you're telling it: only bid on people your models think will fill a form in. Choose Traffic and you're telling it: bid on people who click things. Clickers and form-fillers are two completely different groups of people, and you pay for the one you asked for.

The build, part two — ad set level

  1. Conversion locationWebsite if you're sending to your funnel page — then choose your dataset and set the performance goal to maximise number of conversions, with conversion event Lead. Choose Instant forms only if you decided that in Module 02.
  2. Location — the setting that matters mostDrop a pin on your venue, not your town. Radius +8 to +15 km (5–10 miles) for a city or suburb; up to 25 km rural. Then check the dropdown says “People living in this location” — the default includes visitors and tourists, who cannot bring their child to your Tuesday session.
  3. Age and gender: 25–50, all gendersYou are targeting parents, not players. Every season somebody targets 8 to 12 year olds and wonders why nothing happens. Children that age aren't on Facebook and can't pay you.
  4. Detailed targeting: leave it emptyYes, really. On a small local radius your audience is already tiny — 20,000 to 150,000 people. Stacking “interested in football” on top starves delivery and pushes costs up. Meta's Advantage+ audience reads your creative and your pixel far better than an interest list does.
  5. Exclude your existing parentsUpload your customer list as a custom audience and exclude it. Nothing annoys a paying parent like being sold a free trial for the thing they already pay for — and you're paying for the privilege.
  6. Placements: Advantage+ (all of them)Restricting to Feed only is the classic beginner move, and it raises costs 30–50%. Reels and Stories are cheap inventory. You already made vertical creative — let it run everywhere.
  7. Attribution: 7-day click, 1-day viewParents don't decide instantly. They see it Tuesday and sign up Thursday night from the sofa. A 1-day window makes good ads look dead.
  8. Schedule: all day, every day, starting Monday 00:00At £10–15/day there isn't enough volume for dayparting to do anything except cost you reach. Starting Monday makes your weekly reporting line up cleanly, which matters more than it sounds.

Audience size sanity check

Ads Manager shows an estimated audience size as you build. Under 15,000 and your costs will climb and your frequency will run away — widen the radius or the age range. Over 500,000 in a local academy context and you're probably reaching people who'd never drive to your venue — tighten it. The sweet spot for a local academy is roughly 30,000 to 200,000.

04Loading your creative

You've made the assets. This is which one goes in which slot, and what breaks them.

Meta will happily take one square video and stretch it across nine placements, cropping your captions off in five of them. Ninety seconds of care here is worth more than another day of filming.

PlacementRatioWhat to do
Feed (FB + IG)4:5Takes the most vertical screen space feed allows. This is your main upload.
Stories & Reels9:16Use Edit per placement to swap in your full-screen cut. Keep text out of the top and bottom 14% — the interface covers it.
Right column / search1:1Low volume. A square version keeps it from looking broken.
Video length15–30 secHook in the first 2 seconds. Most people never turn the sound on — burn the captions in.
Thumbnail—Pick the frame with a face and a ball in it. Never a logo card.
Static images4:5Keep text on the image light. Heavy text still suppresses reach in practice.

How many, and what mix

The starting four

  • Session footage — kids playing, noise, energy. The workhorse.
  • Parent testimonial — 20 seconds, filmed on a phone, at the side of a pitch.
  • You to camera — who you are, what the session is, one line of proof.
  • Static offer card — date, venue, age group, big and clear.

Rotation rules

  • Load all 3–4 into one ad set and let Meta find the winner.
  • Expect one ad to take 70–90% of the spend. That's the system working, not a bug.
  • Swap in fresh creative when frequency passes 3–4, not on a fixed schedule.
  • When you swap, change one ad at a time so you can tell what happened.
  • Keep every retired ad in a folder. Winners come back around 6 months later.

The only creative note in this workbook

Put the place name in the first two seconds — on screen and in the voice. “Free trial in Inverurie this Saturday” stops the right parent and lets everybody else scroll past, which is exactly what you want. Local beats slick, every single time.

Tracking your links

Paste this into the URL parameters box at ad level. The double-brace parts fill themselves in automatically.

utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid&utm_campaign={{campaign.name}}&utm_content={{ad.name}}

Now, when a parent says “I saw you online somewhere”, your form data tells you exactly which ad it was. Over a season this is how you learn which creative brings players rather than clicks.

  • The Instagram account is connected, so the ad runs there too.
  • No spelling mistakes in the first line. That line does 80% of the work.

Rejections that hit academies

Almost every academy rejection comes from one policy: personal attributes. Meta doesn't allow you to imply you know something personal about the reader — including things about their child.

What trips itWhyWrite this instead
“Is your child struggling with confidence?”Personal attributes“We help kids build confidence on the pitch.”
“Is your son being overlooked at his club?”Personal attributes“Extra touches for players who want more game time.”
“Unfit or overweight kids welcome”Health and body image“All abilities welcome — no experience needed.”
Before and after body imagesBanned outrightSession footage, faces, parents watching
Club badges and crests you don't ownTrademark / IPYour own branding only
“FIFA”, “Premier League”, pro club namesTrademarkDescribe the standard without borrowing the brand
“Guaranteed to get scouted”Unrealistic outcomes“Players from our sessions have gone on to…”

If an ad gets rejected

  • Request one review. Roughly half get overturned within a day, and the automated first pass is often just wrong.
  • Don't delete and re-upload the same creative repeatedly. Repeated rejections put the whole ad account at risk, and recovering a disabled account is genuinely painful.
  • If it's rejected twice, rewrite the copy rather than arguing. Nine times in ten it's one sentence with the word “your child” in it.
  • Check Account Quality in Business Suite to see if the issue is the ad or the account.

Rewriting the same idea, legally

The trick is to move the sentence from them to us. “Is your child shy?” becomes “A lot of the kids who join us start out shy.” Identical meaning, identical emotional pull, no policy breach — because you're describing your sessions, not making a claim about the reader.

Safeguarding & data — the part ads training skips

This isn't Meta policy. It's the bit that protects your reputation, and one angry parent in a local Facebook group costs more than a campaign ever made you.

Using children in creative

  • Written parental consent for any child who is identifiable. Keep it on file with a date.
  • Keep a consent register — name, date, what it covers, and how to withdraw it.
  • Check the register again before you scale an ad to a wider radius.
  • Never name a child in ad copy or in a comment reply.
  • Never pair a child's image with an exact venue and exact session time in the same post.
  • If a parent withdraws consent, pull the ad that day. Not next week.

Handling the data you collect

  • Say on the form what you'll do with their details, in one line.
  • Link a simple privacy policy. Instant Forms require one — the URL must work.
  • Keep leads in one place, not scattered across three phones.
  • Delete leads that never converted after a reasonable period.
  • If you upload a customer list to Meta, you need a lawful basis and to have told parents you may do it.
  • Don't put a child's medical or SEN details anywhere near a lead form.

“I've turned down two brilliant bits of footage because I couldn't put my hand on the consent. It stung both times. It's still the right call — you're advertising to the same few thousand families you'll be coaching for the next decade, and trust is the whole business.”

Faces you can always use safely

  • You, to camera, talking about the sessions.
  • Your coaching staff, with their permission.
  • Parents giving a testimonial — adults, consenting, on the record.
  • Wide shots and back-of-head footage where no child is identifiable.
  • Your own kids, if you're comfortable with it. Many owners aren't — that's fine too.

05Launch day

Twelve checks, ten minutes. Every one of these is a mistake somebody has already paid for.
  • Objective is Leads — not Traffic, not Engagement, not a boosted post.
  • Location is a pin on the venue with a radius, set to people living in this location.

Module 06 · The first 7 daysASCEND · The Ads Engine

06The first 7 days

The part almost everybody gets wrong: doing nothing, on purpose.

When a new ad set launches it enters the learning phase. Meta is spending your money buying information about who converts. It needs roughly 50 conversion events in 7 days to stabilise. Until then the numbers bounce around wildly — and every edit you make restarts the whole thing, throwing away what it learned and what you paid for it.

Days 1–3: hands off

  • Do not change the budget.
  • Do not pause an ad because it looks bad.
  • Do not add or swap creative.
  • Do not touch the audience or the radius.
  • Do not duplicate it “to test something”.

Do: answer every lead within five minutes, and reply to every comment.

Days 4–7: your first real read

Now you look — and take one action, not five:

  • Leads at or under target cost → leave it completely alone.
  • Leads coming but expensive → give it three more days; costs usually fall as it exits learning.
  • Spent 3× your target cost per lead with zero leads → something is broken. Module 08.
  • One ad taking 80% of spend → normal and good. Meta found your winner.

Day one costs lie

A £22 cost per lead on day one routinely settles to £5 by day four. Judging an ad after twelve hours is judging noise, and the owners who do it kill good campaigns weekly, then conclude that ads don't work for academies.

What normal looks like, day by day

DayWhat you should expect to see
1Spend starts slowly, impressions build through the day, costs look alarming. Ignore.
2–3First leads arrive, often in clusters. Cost per lead swings between double and half your target.
4–5Meta starts favouring one ad. Cost per lead begins settling into a range.
6–7The number you see now is roughly the number you have. This is the one to judge.

The 20% rule, and the label you can ignore

The 20% rule

Once you're out of learning, budget changes bigger than about 20% at a time re-trigger the learning phase. So scale in steps — £15, then £18, then £21 — leaving 3 to 4 days between each. Doubling a budget overnight is the most reliable way to break an ad that was working perfectly.

“Learning limited” — not a failure

This label means the ad set isn't getting around 50 conversions a week. At £15/day with a £6 lead you will live in “learning limited” permanently, and that is completely fine for a local academy. It's a warning designed for advertisers spending thousands a day. Judge yourself on cost per lead and bums on grass, not on Meta's label.

Things that restart learning (avoid during week one)

  • Budget change over ~20%
  • Changing the audience, location or age range
  • Changing the optimisation event or conversion location
  • Adding or removing ads from the ad set
  • Turning the ad set off and on again
  • Editing the creative or the destination link

What a player is worth

Ads aren't expensive or cheap in the abstract. They're expensive or cheap relative to this number.

Most owners never do this sum, so they panic at a £9 lead and switch the ads off the week before they'd have started printing. Do it once, write it down, and it changes how every future decision feels.

Worked example — Dave's academy

Fee £35 − delivery £14 = £21 profit a month. Stays 10 months → player worth £210. Enrol rate 0.28 → break-even lead = £210 × 0.28 = £58.80. Target = £58.80 ÷ 3 = £19.60. Leads per player = 1 ÷ 0.28 = 3.6.

So Dave's real cost per lead of £5 isn't just fine — it's three times better than his target, and the correct response is to spend more, not to congratulate himself and stop.

If B is bigger than A

Your delivery cost is above your fee, so every extra player loses you money and no advertising can fix that. Stop here and go back to the Pricing Calculator (Traction Stage, Resource 01). Ads pour fuel on whatever engine you have — including a broken one.

Module 08 · When it's not workingASCEND · The Ads Engine

08When it's not working

Find the broken layer in sixty seconds. Read top to bottom — the first symptom that matches is your problem.
Barely any impressions · the budget isn't going out
The delivery is blocked, not the creative.

Audience under ~15k → widen the radius. Ad still in review → wait, it can take 24 hours. Payment method failed → check billing. Schedule set to start in the future → check the dates. A bid cap set → remove it. Account spending limit already reached → raise it.

CPM above £20
You're paying a premium for the audience you asked for.

Radius too tight, or interests stacked on top of a small radius → widen and strip the interests. Frequency over 3–4 in a fortnight → the same people keep seeing it, refresh the creative. Some months are simply expensive — December, and the fortnight before school returns, are brutal auctions everywhere.

Impressions fine, CTR under 0.8%
Creative and audience aren't matching.

The one place this workbook hands back to your creative work. Swap the hook, put the place name in the first two seconds, lead with a face and a pitch rather than a logo. Also ask the blunt question: is the offer appealing to a parent, or is it about you and your badges?

Clicks coming, no leads
The page, not the ad. This is the most common one of all.

Open it on 4G on your phone. Slow? Offer below the fold? Form broken on mobile? Does the ad promise something the page never mentions? And check the pixel is actually firing Lead — sometimes the leads are arriving fine and simply aren't being counted, which sends owners chasing a problem that doesn't exist.

Cost per lead doubled overnight
Almost always an edit, or the calendar.

Did you change something in the last 3 days — budget, creative, audience? You've restarted learning. Did a competitor start advertising, or is it a peak week (early January, late August)? Check frequency too: a sudden jump usually means Meta has run out of fresh people to show it to.

Budget isn't spending in full each day
Meta can't find enough people worth bidding on.

The audience is too small, the optimisation event is too rare, or your creative isn't earning impressions in the auction. Widen the radius first, then check that the Lead event is actually firing — an ad set optimising for an event that never happens will throttle itself.

Module 08 · When it's not workingASCEND · The Ads Engine
Leads coming, nobody turns up
Speed of follow-up, nine times out of ten.

How long between the form and your first message? Over an hour is your answer. Then: are you sending a day-before reminder? Is the venue, the entrance and the parking crystal clear? Did you ever actually speak to a human, or only text? Module 09.

They turn up, they don't join
The session, or far more likely the ask at the end of it.

Did you speak to the parent for two minutes at pickup? Did you invite them to join, with a price and a next date, and then stop talking? Did the child leave saying they loved it? No ad campaign in the world fixes a trial session with no ask at the end.

It worked for three weeks, now it's dying
Creative fatigue in a small audience.

Check frequency. Over 3–4 in a small radius means your whole area has seen it several times over. Rotate in fresh creative and change nothing else. This is exactly why you make creative in batches rather than one perfect film.

Leads are junk — wrong ages, wrong towns, timewasters
Your qualifying is happening after the click instead of before it.

Put the age group and the venue in the ad itself, not just on the page. Add the age question to the form. If you're on Instant Forms, switch to “higher intent” or move to your funnel page. Cheap leads that don't fit are not cheap.

The one-change rule

Whatever you diagnose: change one thing, then wait three days. Change three things at once and you've learned nothing, whatever happens next — and you'll be back here next month with the same problem and no more knowledge than today.

Before you blame the ads, check these three

  • Is the Lead event still firing? Pixel Helper, thirty seconds.
  • Are lead alerts still arriving on your phone? They break silently.
  • Is the page still live and fast? Domains expire, hosts fall over, forms break after updates.

09Delivering the lead

The ad's job ends at the form. This module is where the money is actually made or lost.

A lead is not a customer. It's a parent who half-decided something while scrolling, and whose interest starts decaying the moment they close the app. Speed beats polish, every single time. A scruffy text in four minutes outperforms a beautiful email tomorrow.

Time to first contactWhat happens
Under 5 minutesYou catch most of them while the phone is still in their hand
Under 1 hourStill decent. Treat this as your minimum standard.
Same dayYou've lost a chunk of them
Next day or laterMost are gone — and some have booked with someone else

Set this up before you launch, not after

  • Lead alerts as a push notification on your phone, not just an email.
  • One named person responsible, with a backup for coaching hours.
  • An automatic instant reply that buys you the evening if you're on the pitch.
  • The scripts on the next two pages saved in your phone as text shortcuts, so a reply takes ten seconds.

Why text or WhatsApp first, not a call

Parents of young children do not answer unknown numbers — they assume it's a scam or a school problem. They read texts within minutes. Text first to open the door, then call once you're a name rather than a number.

The contact sequence — part one

Change the wording so it sounds like you. Don't change the timings — the timings are the part that works.

  1. Instantly — automatic reply
    “Hi [name], thanks for booking [child] in for the free trial at [venue] — it's [day] at [time]. I'll message you personally shortly to confirm. Any questions in the meantime, just reply here. [Your name], [Academy].”
  2. Within 5 minutes — personal text or WhatsApp
    “Hi [name] — [your name] from [Academy]. Just seen [child]'s booking for Saturday. Quick one: what age is [child], and have they played much before? Just want to put them in the right group.”

    A question they can answer in three words. It starts a conversation and confirms the booking without asking for a commitment.

  3. Within 1 hour — phone call, if no replySay who you are, confirm the spot, say what to bring. Ninety seconds, no pitch. If they don't answer, leave a voicemail and send a text saying you've called — the text is what gets read.
  4. Day before — the reminder
    “Looking forward to seeing [child] tomorrow at [time], [venue] — we're on the [pitch/hall]. Just boots, shin pads and a water bottle. Parking is [detail]. See you there.”

    This single message is worth 15–20 points of show-up rate. It is the highest-return message in the whole sequence.

  5. Morning of — short nudge
    “Morning! All set for [time] today. Text me if you're running late — no problem at all.”

    “No problem at all” matters: the number one reason a family no-shows is that they're running late and feel too awkward to walk in.

Make these one-tap on your phone

  • iPhone: Settings → General → Keyboard → Text Replacement. Save each script with a shortcut like ;trial.
  • Android: Settings → System → Languages & input → Personal dictionary.
  • WhatsApp Business: use Quick Replies (/trial) and set the away message as your auto-reply.
  • Then a five-minute response takes ten seconds, which is the only way it survives a busy week.

Tone rules

  • Write like a coach texting a parent, not like a business emailing a customer.
  • One question per message. Two questions gets you zero answers.
  • Use the child's name in every message. It's their child, not a lead.
  • No emojis in the first message, no pressure, no capital-letter urgency.
  • Never send a wall of text. Three lines maximum.

The contact sequence — part two

  1. Right after the session — the ask, face to face
    “[Child] did really well today — especially [specific thing they did]. We'd love to have them regularly. It's £[price] a month, same time every week. Shall I get them signed up for next Saturday?”

    Then stop talking. Let them answer. This is the whole ballgame, and it's the step most owners skip because it feels pushy. It isn't — they came to you.

  2. Same evening — the link
    “Great to meet you today. Here's the sign-up for [child] — takes two minutes: [link]. Any questions just shout.”
  3. +2 days — one soft chase
    “Hi [name], just checking whether [child] fancied coming back this week? Happy to hold their spot until Friday if that helps.”
  4. +7 days — close it off warmly
    “No problem at all if the timing isn't right. I'll keep you posted when we've got space or a camp coming up — door's always open.”

    Then stop, and tag them for the next camp. Chasing beyond this costs goodwill and gains nothing.

The two messages that recover lost money

No-show, same evening

“Missed you at [venue] today — everything alright? Want me to put [child] in for this Saturday instead?”

No guilt, no lecture. A big share of no-shows rebook off this one message.

Attended, went quiet

“Hi [name] — was [child] keen after Saturday? We've got two spots left in that group this term, wanted to check before they go.”

Real scarcity only. If there aren't two spots left, don't say there are.

Never let a lead die

The four rules

  • Every lead goes on the board the day it arrives, with a status and a next action date. Your Academy Leads base does this for you.
  • A no-show is not dead. One message, no guilt, offer the next date.
  • “Not right now” means next camp, not never. Tag them and message when it opens.
  • Five minutes a week scanning for anything with no next action set. That's where the money hides.

Pipeline statuses

  • New — just arrived, not yet contacted
  • Contacted — you've messaged, no reply yet
  • Trial booked — date agreed
  • Trial attended — they showed up
  • Enrolled — paying
  • Lost — with a one-word reason: price, timing, distance, ghosted

The reasons matter. Three “distance” in a month means your radius is too wide.

Comments and DMs are leads too

  • Reply to every comment, even “how much is it?” — publicly first, then DM.
  • Answer the price question straight. Dodging it in public reads as expensive.
  • Hide, don't delete, anything abusive. Hiding is invisible to them; deleting starts a fight.
  • Tagged friends (“@Claire look at this”) are gold — reply to both of them.
  • Comments and reactions are social proof that lowers your CPM. An engaged ad literally costs less to run, so replying is a performance tactic, not just manners.

Your response promise

Write it down, then hold yourself to it. Everything in Module 07 depends on this one line.

For example: “Every lead gets a personal text within 30 minutes between 8am and 9pm, and by 9am the next morning otherwise. Sarah covers Tuesday and Thursday evenings.”

The Monday five-minute lead sweep

  • Anything still New after 24 hours — contact it now
  • Anything with no next action date — set one
  • Last week's no-shows — one message each
  • Last week's attended but not enrolled — the two-day chase
  • Anything over a fortnight old with no reply — close it off warmly

Three messages never to send

  • “Just following up…” with nothing new in it. Give them a reason to reply.
  • A price list as a first reply. Answer the price, then get them to the session.
  • Anything that reads as annoyed. They're a busy parent, not a bad person — and they talk to other parents.

Module 10 · The weekly rhythmASCEND · The Ads Engine

10The weekly rhythm

Fifteen minutes, once a week, same day, same time. This is the entire ongoing job.

The owners who get results aren't the ones who check Ads Manager six times a day — those ones fiddle, restart learning, and wonder why nothing settles. Set an alarm for Monday morning and do exactly this.

  1. Open Ads Manager, last 7 daysWrite down four numbers in the same place every week: spend, leads, cost per lead, frequency. Same four, every week, forever.
  2. Compare to your target cost per leadThe number from your worksheet — not a number you have a feeling about.
  3. Check frequencyUnder 2.5 is fine. Over 3.5 in a small radius, plan a creative swap this week.
  4. Take at most one actionScale up 20%, swap one creative, or do nothing. One. Multiple changes at once and you never learn which one did it.
  5. Check the lead boardAnything with no next action, deal with it now. Anything older than a fortnight with no reply, close it off warmly.
  6. Count the real outcomeHow many trials booked, attended and enrolled last week? That's the scoreboard. Ads Manager is just engine noise.

What not to do the other six days

Don't check the dashboard daily. Don't pause an ad on a bad afternoon. Don't ask three people in a Facebook group what they think of your CPM. Answer your leads, coach your sessions, and look on Monday.

Once a quarter, do this too

  • Refresh your creative library — film a batch, not a masterpiece.
  • Re-upload your customer list so exclusions and lookalikes stay current.
  • Check the funnel page still loads fast and the form still works.
  • Look back over three months of the tracker overleaf and ask what actually changed.

Module 11 · Scaling & retargetingASCEND · The Ads Engine

11Scaling & retargeting

Only once a campaign has hit target cost per lead for two straight weeks. Not before.

How to scale without breaking it

  • Vertical: +20% budget every 3–4 days, while cost per lead holds. Slow, boring, reliable.
  • Horizontal: duplicate the ad set with a different location — a second venue, a neighbouring town. Same creative, same settings. This is how a local academy actually grows: more places, not more money in one place.
  • Capacity first: check you can physically take the players before you turn the tap up.
  • When cost per lead rises as you scale: hold at the last budget that worked for a week. Small radiuses have a natural ceiling — find it and sit just under it.

When to kill something

  • Spent 3× target cost per lead, zero leads, and Module 08 found nothing broken → kill the ad, keep the ad set.
  • Cost per lead more than double target for 7 days after learning → pause and rebuild with different creative.
  • Frequency over 5 and CTR falling → the area is saturated. Rest it two weeks.
  • Never kill during learning. You're binning an incomplete experiment you've already paid for.

Retargeting — the cheapest leads you'll ever buy

Wait until you have 30 days of traffic, then add one small ad set at £3–5/day aimed only at people who already know you. These convert several times better than cold, because the ad is a nudge rather than an introduction.

Always exclude current customers from cold ads

Nothing annoys a paying parent like being advertised a free trial for the thing they already pay you for — and you are paying money for the privilege of irritating them. Refresh the uploaded list every quarter.

The referral ad nobody runs

Point a £3/day ad at your existing parents only, with one message: “Know a family who'd love this? Bring a friend to any session this month.” It's the cheapest new-player source you have, and it barely costs anything to reach an audience that small.

Every term Meta uses that sounds like it means something else.
TermWhat it actually means
Ad accountThe billing container your campaigns live in. Owned by your business portfolio.

Attribution windowHow long after seeing or clicking an ad a conversion still counts as caused by it.
Business portfolioFormerly Business Manager. The top-level container that owns your Page, ad account and datasets.
CAPIConversions API. Server-side tracking that survives browser blocking. Recovers conversions the pixel loses.
CPCCost per click. Use the link click version, not “all clicks”, which counts people expanding your text.
CPMCost per 1,000 impressions. The price of attention in your area this week.
CTRClick-through rate. Clicks divided by impressions. Again, use the link version.

DatasetThe new name for the pixel. The tracking code on your funnel page.
FrequencyAverage number of times each person saw your ad. Over 3–4 in a small area means fatigue.
ImpressionsTimes your ad was shown. Not people — one person can be several impressions.
