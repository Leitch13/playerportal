Read this first

What the training covers, and the one idea it’s built on.

Most academy owners think ads fail because the video wasn’t good enough. It’s almost never the video. It’s the plumbing behind it — the wrong objective, no tracking, a broken landing page, a lead that sat in an inbox for two days. That’s the delivery side, and that’s what this is.

Every result you get is one of five layers doing its job or failing. Ads Manager will not tell you which. This training teaches you to read the numbers and know instantly which layer is broken.

01

The plumbing

Do this once. 30–45 minutes. Skip a step here and you’ll be debugging it for a month.

Meta has two different places to click: Meta Business Suite (the admin layer — assets, people, payments) and Ads Manager (where campaigns get built). You need the first set up properly before the second is worth touching.

A. Business portfolio & ad account

  1. Create a Business portfolioGo to business.facebook.com. Create one in your academy’s name, not your own. This is the container that owns everything — if you ever hand the academy on, sell it, or bring in help, you hand over this, not your personal login.
  2. Add your Page to itBusiness settings → Accounts → Pages → Add → Add an existing Page. If your Page is stuck under a personal profile, this is the step that frees it.
  3. Create an ad accountBusiness settings → Accounts → Ad accounts → Add → Create a new ad account. Name it ACADEMY NAME - Ads, currency GBP, time zone London.
  4. Warning: currency and time zone are permanentYou cannot change them later without creating a whole new ad account and losing all your data. Get them right now.
  5. Add a payment methodBilling → Payment settings. Add a card. Set a spending limit of your monthly budget while you’re learning — it’s a hard stop that protects you from a mis-typed budget.
  6. Turn on two-factor authenticationHacked ad accounts are common and Meta support is close to non-existent. 2FA on the personal profile that admins the business. Do it now, not later.

B. Tracking — the dataset (pixel)

The pixel (Meta now calls it a dataset) is a snippet of code on your funnel page that tells Meta “this person filled the form in.” Without it, Meta is guessing who to show your ads to, and you are flying blind on cost per lead.

  1. Create the datasetBusiness settings → Data sources → Datasets → Add. Name it ACADEMY NAME - Web. Copy the ID (a 15–16 digit number) somewhere safe.
  2. Install it on your funnel pageIf you’re on the ASCEND trial funnel: paste the ID into the pixel field in your page setup. If you’re on Player Portal: Settings → Marketing → Meta Pixel, paste the ID, save — it fires page views and lead events for you automatically.
  3. Wire the Lead eventThe form submission must fire a Lead event. This is the single most important line of tracking you own — it’s what “cost per lead” is counted from and what Meta optimises towards.
  4. Test it before you spend a pennyInstall the Meta Pixel Helper Chrome extension. Load your funnel page — it should show PageView. Submit a test lead — it should show Lead. If it doesn’t, stop and fix it. Everything downstream is built on this.
  5. Verify your domainBusiness settings → Brand safety → Domains. Add your domain and verify it (DNS TXT record or a meta tag in your page head). This gives you control of your own conversion events and stops iOS privacy rules eating your data.
  6. Set the Lead event as a priority eventEvents Manager → your dataset → Aggregated Event Measurement → configure web events. Put Lead at the top. Ignore this and iOS users’ conversions may not report at all.
If you use Meta Instant Forms insteadYou don’t need a pixel for the ad to work — the form is on Facebook itself. You still want it for retargeting later. See Module 02 for the trade-off; it’s a real decision, not a shortcut.

C. Where the leads land

Decide this now, in writing, before a single ad runs. A lead that arrives somewhere nobody looks is money set on fire.

Funnel page leads
Email alert to your phone + row in your Academy Leads base (Airtable) + WhatsApp notification if you’ve wired it.
Instant Form leads
They sit in Meta Business Suite → Leads Centre and nobody gets an email by default. You must connect them out (Zapier / Make / a CRM) or check the Leads Centre twice a day. This is where most academies lose leads.
Who is on the hook
One named person. If it’s “whoever sees it first”, it’s nobody.
02

One offer, one destination

The two decisions that cap your results before the ad ever runs.

You are not advertising your academy. Nobody clicks “an academy.” You are advertising one specific thing a parent can say yes to this week.

Offers that convert cold traffic

  • Free trial session — named date, named venue, named age group. The workhorse. Highest volume, lowest friction.
  • Holiday camp — deadline built in, parents are actively searching, easiest paid conversion of the year.
  • £10 taster block (2–3 weeks) — fewer leads than a free trial but a far higher show-up rate, because they’ve paid something.
  • New venue / new age group launch — genuine news, works brilliantly for a 2–3 week burst.

Offers that don’t

  • “Join our academy” — no date, no decision, no urgency.
  • “Sign up for our newsletter” — a parent whose kid wants to play football does not want a newsletter.
  • Anything with a price as the headline before they know who you are.
  • Three offers in one ad. One campaign, one offer. Always.

Where you send the click

Lead qualityHigher — they chose to click throughLower — thumb-slip signups are common

The ASCEND defaultUse your funnel page. You pay a bit more per lead and you get a parent who actually turns up — and cost per enrolled player is the only number that pays your bills. Use Instant Forms when you’re filling a camp fast, on a deadline, and volume beats quality.
If you use an Instant FormChoose “Higher intent”, not “More volume”, and add one custom question (“What age is your child?” with age-band options). That one question filters out half the accidental leads and pre-qualifies the call.

Pre-flight your destination page

Before you spend, open your funnel page on your phone, on 4G with wifi off, and check:

  • It loads in under 3 seconds. Every extra second costs roughly 10% of your leads.
  • The offer, the age group, the venue and the date are visible without scrolling.
  • The form asks for 4 fields maximum: parent name, child age, phone, email. Every extra field drops completion.
  • The phone field is set to a phone keypad type. Sounds trivial — it isn’t.
  • You submitted a real test lead, and you got the alert on your phone.
  • The thank-you page tells them exactly what happens next: “I’ll text you within the hour to confirm your spot.”
03

Build the campaign

Click by click. Meta renames buttons — the function stays the same, so read what each setting does.

Three levels, and they each control something different. Get this straight and Ads Manager stops being confusing forever.

Campaign

Sets the goal and (usually) the budget. What are you asking Meta to go and get?

Ad set

Sets who, where, how much, when. Audience, location, placements, schedule.

Ad

The creative itself — video, text, headline, button, destination link.

Step by step

  1. Ads Manager → CreateObjective: Leads. Not Traffic. Not Engagement. Not the “Boost” button on your post. Leads tells Meta’s algorithm to hunt for people who fill forms in, not people who like things.
  2. Campaign nameUse a convention you’ll still understand in six months: 2026-09 | Trial | Aberdeen U8-U12. Sloppy names are why owners can’t tell what worked last year.
  3. Special ad category: leave it OFFThat’s for housing, employment, credit and politics. Children’s sport is not one — switching it on needlessly strips out your age and radius targeting and wrecks delivery.
  4. Budget: at campaign level (Advantage campaign budget)Set £10–15/day to start. One budget, shared across ad sets, Meta moves it to whatever’s working. Simpler and better than splitting it yourself at this spend level.
  5. Conversion locationWebsite if you’re sending to your funnel page — then pick your dataset and set the performance goal to maximise number of conversions, conversion event Lead. Choose Instant forms only if you decided that in Module 02.
  6. Location targeting — the one that matters mostDrop a pin on your venue, not your town. Set a radius of +8 to +15 km (5–10 miles). Rural academies: go wider, up to 25 km. Make sure the dropdown says “People living in this location” — the default includes tourists and visitors.
  7. Age & gender25–50, all genders. You are targeting parents, not players. Every season someone targets 8–12 year olds and wonders why nothing happens — kids that age aren’t on Facebook and can’t pay you.
  8. Detailed targeting: leave it emptyYes, really. On a small local radius your audience is already tiny (20k–150k people). Stacking “interested in football” on top starves delivery and pushes your costs up. Let Meta’s Advantage+ audience find the parents — it reads your creative and your pixel far better than an interest list does.
  9. Placements: Advantage+ placements (all of them)Restricting to Feed only is the classic beginner move and it raises your costs 30–50%. Reels and Stories are cheap. You already made vertical creative — let it run everywhere.
  10. Attribution setting7-day click, 1-day view. Parents don’t decide instantly — they see it Tuesday and sign up Thursday from the sofa. A 1-day window will make good ads look dead.
  11. ScheduleRun all day, every day. At £10–15/day there isn’t enough volume for dayparting to do anything but cost you reach. Start the campaign at 00:00 on a Monday so your weekly reporting lines up cleanly.
  12. Number of ads: 3 to 4You already have the creative. Load 3–4 into one ad set and let Meta pick the winner. Do not build one ad set per creative at this budget — you’ll split your data so thin that nothing ever learns.
The shape you’re aiming for1 campaign → 1 ad set → 3–4 ads. That’s it. Complexity is not sophistication at £15/day — it’s just less data per box and slower learning.

Safeguarding — the bit ads training usually skips

  • Written parental consent for any child identifiable in your creative. Keep it on file. This isn’t a Meta rule, it’s a you rule — and one angry parent in a local Facebook group costs more than the campaign made.
  • Never name a child in ad copy or in a comment reply.
  • Never post the exact address and exact session time for a specific named child.
  • Check your consent list before you scale a winning ad to a wider radius.
05

Launch day

Twelve checks. Ten minutes. Every one of these is a mistake somebody has already paid for.
  • Objective is Leads — not Traffic, not Engagement, not a boosted post.
  • Location is a pin on the venue with a radius, set to people living in this location.
  • Age range is 25–50 (parents), all genders.
  • Daily budget is the number you meant — read it twice. £15 and £150 look similar at speed.
  • Account spending limit is set as a hard stop.
  • Placements are Advantage+ (all), with a 9:16 cut loaded for Stories and Reels.
  • Pixel Helper shows PageView and Lead firing on your funnel page.
  • You clicked your own ad link on your phone and the page loaded correctly, on 4G.
  • You submitted a real test lead and the alert reached your phone.
  • The date, venue and age group in the ad match what’s on the page — exactly.
  • You have time in your diary to answer leads for the next 7 days.
  • Page comment notifications are ON — parents ask questions in the comments, and a question ignored for two days is a lead lost in public.
John says“The night before a launch I still send myself a test lead from my own phone. Every single time. It’s caught a broken form twice, and each of those would’ve been a week of spend into a dead end.”
06

The first 7 days

The part almost everybody gets wrong: doing nothing, on purpose.

When a new ad set launches it enters the learning phase. Meta is spending your money buying information about who converts. It needs roughly 50 conversion events in 7 days to stabilise. Until then the numbers bounce wildly, and every edit you make restarts the whole thing, throwing away what it learned and what you paid for it.

Days 1–3: hands off

  • Do not change the budget.
  • Do not pause an ad because it “looks bad”.
  • Do not add or swap creative.
  • Do not touch the audience.
  • Do answer every lead within 5 minutes.
  • Do reply to every comment on the ad.
Day 1 costs lieA £22 cost per lead on day one routinely settles to £5 by day four. Judging an ad after 12 hours is judging noise.

Days 4–7: first real read

Now you look. Compare against the benchmarks in Module 07 and take one action, not five:

  • Leads coming in at or under target CPL → leave it completely alone. It’s working.
  • Leads coming, but expensive → leave it another 3 days. It often improves as it exits learning.
  • Spend over 3× your target CPL with zero leads → something is broken. Module 08.
  • One ad has taken 80%+ of spend → that’s normal and good. Meta found your winner.

The 20% rule

Once you’re out of learning, budget changes over about 20% at a time re-trigger the learning phase. So scale in steps: £15 → £18 → £21, leaving 3–4 days between each. Doubling a budget overnight is the most reliable way to break an ad that was working.

“Learning limited”This label means the ad set isn’t getting ~50 conversions a week. At £15/day and a £6 lead you’ll live in “learning limited” permanently — and that is completely fine for a local academy. It is a warning, not a failure. Judge on cost per lead and bums on grass, not on Meta’s label.

The funnel maths, all the way to the bank

Track these four conversion steps every month. Where the drop-off is tells you what to fix — and it is very rarely the ad.

StepHealthyIf it’s below
Lead → contacted100% within 1 hourIt’s you, not the ads. Module 09.
Lead → trial booked60–80%Your follow-up is too slow or too soft
Booked → turned up60–75%You’re not reminding them the day before
Turned up → enrolled50–70%The session or the ask at the end needs work

Run those together: 100 leads → 70 booked → 47 attend → 28 enrol. At £5 a lead that’s £500 spent for 28 players — about £18 to acquire a player who pays you monthly for years.

08

When it’s not working

Find the broken layer in 60 seconds. Read the symptoms top to bottom — the first one that matches is your problem.
Barely any impressions · spend not going out
The delivery layer is blocked, not the creative.

Audience too small (under ~15k in radius) → widen it. Ad still in review → wait, it can take 24h. Payment method failed → check billing. Schedule set to start in the future → check the dates. Bid cap set → remove it.

High CPM (£20+)
You’re paying a premium for the audience you asked for.

Radius too tight, or interest stacking on top of a small radius → widen the radius, strip the interests. Same people seeing it repeatedly (frequency over 3–4 in a fortnight) → refresh creative. December and late August are genuinely expensive months — sometimes it’s just the calendar.

Impressions fine, CTR under 0.8%
Creative and audience aren’t matching.

The one place this training defers to your creative work: swap in a different hook, lead with the location in the first 2 seconds, put a real face and a real pitch on screen. Also check the obvious — is the offer in the ad actually appealing to a parent, or is it about you?

Clicks coming, no leads
The page, not the ad. This is the most common one.

Load it on 4G on your phone. Is it slow? Is the offer above the fold? Does the form work? Does the ad promise something the page doesn’t mention? Check the pixel is actually firing Lead — sometimes the leads are arriving and simply aren’t being counted.

Leads coming, nobody turns up
Speed of follow-up, nine times out of ten.

How long between form submission and your first message? If it’s over an hour, that’s your answer. Also: are you sending a day-before reminder? Is the venue and parking crystal clear? Module 09.

They turn up, they don’t join
The session or the ask — not the ads.

Did you speak to the parent for two minutes at the end? Did you actually invite them to join, with a price and a next date? Did the child have a great time and say so in the car? Ads cannot fix a trial session with no ask at the end of it.

It worked for 3 weeks, now it’s dying
Creative fatigue in a small audience.

Check frequency. Over 3–4 in a small radius means your whole area has seen it several times. Rotate in fresh creative from your library, keep everything else identical. This is why you make creative in batches.

09

Delivering the lead

The ad’s job ends at the form. This module is where the money is actually made or lost.

A lead is not a customer. It’s a parent who half-decided something while scrolling, and whose interest starts decaying the second they close the app. Speed beats polish, every time.

The 5-minute rule

Contact rates fall off a cliff with time. The numbers across every industry that’s ever measured this are brutal:

Time to first contactRoughly what happens
Under 5 minutesYou catch most of them while the phone is still in their hand
Under 1 hourStill decent — the minimum acceptable standard
Same dayYou’ve lost a chunk of them
Next day or laterMost are gone, and they’ve often booked with someone else
Set this up before you launchLead alert to your phone as a push notification, not just an email. Then either you or one named person answers it. If you coach 6–8pm, either an assistant handles the window or you set an auto-reply that buys you the evening (below).

The contact sequence

Text or WhatsApp first — parents of young kids don’t answer unknown numbers, but they read texts. Adapt the wording to sound like you; the timings are the part that matters.

  1. Instant — automatic reply“Hi [name], thanks for booking [child] in for the free trial at [venue]. It’s [day] at [time]. I’ll message you personally shortly to confirm — any questions in the meantime, just reply here. John, [Academy].”
  2. Within 5 minutes — personal text/WhatsApp“Hi [name] — [your name] from [Academy]. Just seen [child]’s booking for Saturday. Quick one: what age is [child], and have they played much before? Wanted to make sure I put them in the right group.” A question they can answer in three words starts a conversation and confirms the booking without asking for a commitment.
  3. Within 1 hour — phone call, if no replySay who you are, confirm the spot, tell them what to bring. Ninety seconds. If they don’t answer, leave a voicemail and text saying you’ve called.
  4. Day before — reminder“Looking forward to seeing [child] tomorrow at [time], [venue] — we’re on the [pitch/hall]. Just boots, shin pads and a water bottle. Parking is [detail]. See you there.” This single message is worth 15–20 points of show-up rate.
  5. Morning of — short nudge“Morning! All set for [time] today. Text me if you’re running late — no problem at all.”
  6. Immediately after the session — the askSpeak to the parent at pickup, face to face: “[Child] did really well today — especially [specific thing]. We’d love to have them regularly. It’s £[price] a month, same time every week. Shall I get them signed up for next Saturday?” Then stop talking. This is the whole ballgame and most owners skip it.
  7. Same evening — the link“Great to meet you today. Here’s the sign-up link for [child] — takes two minutes: [link]. Any questions just shout.”
  8. +2 days — one soft chase“Hi [name], just checking whether [child] fancied coming back this week? Happy to hold their spot until Friday if that helps.”
  9. +7 days — the close-off“No problem if the timing isn’t right — I’ll keep you posted when we’ve got space or a camp coming up. Doors are always open.” Then stop, and put them on the list for the next camp. Chasing past this point costs goodwill and gains nothing.

Never-let-a-lead-die rules

  • Every lead goes on the board the day it arrives, with a status and a next action date. Your Academy Leads base does this.
  • No-shows are not dead. One text: “Missed you Saturday — want me to put [child] in for this week instead?” A big share come back.
  • “Not right now” means next camp, not never. Tag them and message when the camp opens.
  • Weekly: five minutes scanning for anything with no next action set. That’s where the money hides.

Comments are leads too

  • Reply to every comment, even “how much is it?” — publicly, then DM.
  • Answer the price question straight. Dodging it in public reads as expensive.
  • Hide, don’t delete, anything abusive — hiding is invisible to them, deleting starts a fight.
  • Tagged friends (“@Claire look at this”) are gold. Reply to both.
  • Comments and reactions are social proof that lowers your CPM. An engaged ad literally costs less to run.
John says“For years I told myself I’d ring the leads after training. By then it was 9:30pm, I was knackered, and it waited until tomorrow — and tomorrow it was a stranger asking about something they’d forgotten. The academies that grow aren’t the ones with the best ads. They’re the ones that answer first.”

The weekly rhythm

15 minutes, once a week. Same day, same time. This is the whole ongoing job.

Monday, 15 minutes

  1. Open Ads Manager, last 7 daysWrite down four numbers in the same place every week: spend, leads, cost per lead, frequency.
  2. Compare to your target CPLThe number from the calculator in Module 07 — not a number you feel about.
  3. Check frequencyUnder 2.5 is fine. Over 3.5 in a small radius, plan a creative swap this week.
  4. Take at most one actionScale up 20%, swap creative, or nothing. One. Multiple changes at once means you never learn which one did it.
  5. Check the lead boardAnything without a next action, deal with it now.
  6. Count the actual outcomeHow many trials booked, attended, enrolled last week? That’s the scoreboard. Ads Manager is just the engine noise.

11

Scaling & retargeting

Only once you have a campaign hitting target CPL for two straight weeks. Not before.

How to scale without breaking it

  • Vertical: +20% budget every 3–4 days while CPL holds. Slow, boring, reliable.
  • Horizontal: duplicate the ad set with a different location (a second venue or a new town). Same creative, same settings. This is how a local academy actually grows — more places, not more money in one place.
  • Capacity first: before you scale, check you can physically take the players. Filling a session you can’t staff is worse than no ads at all.
  • When CPL rises as you scale: hold at the last budget that worked for a week. There’s a natural ceiling in a small radius — find it, sit under it.

When to kill something

  • Spent 3× your target CPL, zero leads, and Module 08 found nothing broken → kill the ad, keep the ad set.
  • CPL more than double target for 7 days after learning → pause and rebuild with different creative.
  • Frequency over 5 and CTR falling → the area is saturated. Rest it for 2 weeks.
  • Never kill during learning. You’re killing an incomplete experiment you already paid for.

Retargeting — the cheapest leads you’ll ever buy

Wait until you have 30 days of traffic, then add a second, small ad set (£3–5/day) aimed only at people who already know you. These audiences convert several times better than cold, because the ad is a nudge, not an introduction.
