Your first 30 days

Start here.

Nine steps, in this order. It front-loads the boring bit — getting honest about your numbers — because every good decision after it depends on knowing where you actually are. Budget about three hours across the month. Tick things off as you go; it saves on this device.

0 / 9
Week 1

Get the picture

Nothing to build yet. This week is you finding out the truth about the academy, and me finding it out with you.

Step 15 min

Book your kickoff call

Forty-five minutes, one-to-one. Do this now rather than "when I've had a look round" — the slot you book is usually a week or two out, so booking early means the rest of week one has a deadline attached to it.

Book the kickoff call 45 minutes · video call
Step 210 min

Fill in your intake

Player numbers, prices, what you take a month, what's bugging you most. It means I turn up to the call already knowing your academy, and we spend the time on what to do rather than on you explaining the setup. Estimates are fine — step 4 sharpens them.

Open the intake form Do this before the kickoff call
Step 310 min

Take the diagnostic

Thirty statements across the six stages, scored out of 25 each. It hands you two things: your level, and the one stage that's actually holding you back. Most owners are wrong about which one it is — they're usually working the stage that feels urgent, not the one that's broken.

Take the diagnostic Write your score down — you'll compare in 90 days
Week 2

Face the numbers

This is the week that stings a bit. Do it anyway. Everyone who's grown properly has been through it.

Step 415 min

Run the pricing calculator

Take one session. Put in what you charge, how many turn up, the venue, the coaches, and — this is the bit people leave out — the admin hours it costs you. What comes out the other end is your real hourly rate. If it's under twenty quid, that's not a pricing tweak, that's the whole problem.

Open the calculator Use your worst session, not your best
Step 515 min

Map your money

Zoom out from one session to the whole year. Where you are now, where you'd be on realistic targets for players, price and extra income, and what that gap is worth over twelve months. That number is usually the reason to keep going when the work gets tedious.

Open the money map Screenshot the result
Step 620 min

Audit your week

Paint in where your hours actually went last week — not where you meant them to go. Then split it: what genuinely needs you, and what you're doing because you've always done it. That second list is your growth budget. You can't add players to a week that's already full.

Open the time audit Then the roles audit
Weeks 3 & 4

Fix one thing

You now know more about your academy than you did a fortnight ago. The temptation is to fix all of it. Don't.

Step 7On the call

Pick your one thing

On the kickoff call we agree a single fix for the next 30 days, and how we'll know it worked. One. Not a list of five, because a list of five is a list of none. It'll usually come from your weakest diagnostic stage, and it'll usually be smaller than you expect.

We do this together — tick it once we've agreed it
Step 8Ongoing

Work your stage folder

The library is filed by the six stages. Go to the one the diagnostic flagged as your weakest, and use the material there — not the stuff that looks most interesting. If your Audit score is the lowest, marketing material is a distraction no matter how badly you want more players.

Open the library Start at your weakest stage
Step 9Day 30

Report back

Bring three things to your first monthly call: what you did, what the number did, and what got in the way. Even "it didn't work" is progress if you know why. Bringing nothing is the only version of this that wastes both our time.

Book your monthly call Same time each month works best
