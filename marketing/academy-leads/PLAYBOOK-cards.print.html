Injury

A child is hurt. Calm, quick, in this order.

Stop the activity and make the area safe. Keep the other players busy with a drill away from it.

First aider assesses. Serious injury, or ANY head, neck or back concern — call 999 first, questions later.

Treat and reassure. Keep them warm, keep them talking, keep it low-drama.

Tell the parent. Serious: phone them now. Minor: face to face at pick-up — never let them find out at home.

Never

  • Move a child with a suspected head, neck or back injury
  • Give any medication — including painkillers
  • Let them "run it off" after a head knock
Then: file the reportSame day, from your phone. It takes a minute and it is the academy's official accident record.

Safeguarding concern

A child tells you something, or you see something that worries you. Your only job is to listen and pass it on — today.

Listen calmly. Let them talk at their own pace. Do not ask leading questions — "tell me more" is enough.

Never promise secrecy. Say: "I have to tell someone whose job is to keep you safe."

Reassure them. They did the right thing telling you. It is not their fault.

Write down their exact words as soon as you can — phone notes are fine. Facts, not opinions.

Tell the welfare officer or owner TODAY. Not after the weekend. Today.

Never

  • Investigate it yourself or quiz the child
  • Confront the person involved
  • Discuss it with anyone except the welfare officer / owner
Then: file the reportSpeak to the welfare officer FIRST, then file. Details are restricted to the owner & welfare officer.

Missing child

You cannot see a child who should be there. Minutes matter — act, don't hope.

Shout it to the lead coach now. Child's name and where they were last seen. No embarrassment — a false alarm is a good outcome.

One coach holds the group. Headcount everyone else and keep them in one place.

Everyone else searches now: toilets, exits, car park, last place seen, favourite hiding spots.

Not found within a few minutes? Call 999, then the parents. Do not keep searching quietly instead of calling.

Never

  • Leave the rest of the group unsupervised to join the search
  • Delay the 999 call to "avoid a fuss"
Then: file the reportOnce the child is safe — every detail while it's fresh. The academy reviews every one of these, found-quickly or not.

Angry parent

A parent is raising their voice — at you, another parent, or a referee. De-escalate, don't win.

Move it away from the children. "Let's talk over here" — walking somewhere calms most people on its own.

Stay calm, listen first. Lower your voice; let them empty the tank before you respond.

Don't argue or match their tone. You will not win an argument at a touchline, and you don't need to.

If it continues or turns threatening, end it: "I'm going to get [owner] to call you today" — then walk away and make that call.

Never

  • Have the argument in front of the kids
  • Put hands on anyone, even to guide them away
  • Promise outcomes on the spot ("your child will start next week")
Then: file the reportEven when it fizzled out — patterns across weeks are what the owner needs to see.

[ACADEMY NAME]Playbook · 5 of 5

Coach no-show

You're a coach down and the kids are arriving. Safety first, session second.

Count heads vs adults. Can you run safely at ratio? That question decides everything else.

Merge or simplify. Fewer, bigger groups with simpler drills beats cancelling — if the ratios allow it.

Call the owner now. Don't soldier on in silence — they may be able to send cover inside 20 minutes.

If ratios are impossible, keep every child together in one supervised space, with arriving parents asked to stay, until cover or pick-up.

Never

  • Leave any group unsupervised, even briefly
  • Send children away early without a parent physically there
Then: file the reportStaffing gaps are a business problem the owner fixes with data — give them the data.
